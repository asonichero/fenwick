# The Manor in the Woods: Game Design Document

Nov 18, 2026 · @Sir

## High-level concept and premise

**Genre.** Third-person narrative walking simulator with gated spatial
progression, environmental storytelling, scripted dialogue journeys, and
an ongoing post-game domestic simulation loop.

**Setting.** Fenwick Hall, a neglected country house deep in the
Fenwood, above the estate village of Redmere. A walled garden, a dense
timber perimeter and the village lie within its borders.

**Premise.** A practical, woodsy man in his mid-to-late 30s, Silas
Fenwick, unexpectedly inherits a crumbling woodland estate from a
reclusive great-uncle who shut himself up in the Hall and let it decay.
He arrives with tools, patience and no aristocratic pretensions, and
intends to restore the property. As he unlocks wings of the house and
clears overgrown paths, he meets seven young women from classic European
fairy tales. Their flaws repeatedly put them in danger: carelessness,
naivety, stubborn curiosity, self-neglect, scarcity-driven deceit,
sheltered ignorance and excessive trust. He steps into the role of
employer, guardian and firm authority, and brings order to the estate
and to the lives of the women under his roof.

**Lore layer.** The Redmere series supplies the background: names,
places, village life and Silas's standing. It does not change the cast,
the chapters or the structure above. Where this document adds series
material it says so.

## Core gameplay and structural pillars

- **Third-person spatial exploration.** The player explores the Hall,
  courtyard, woods and village edge on foot at a grounded, deliberate
  pace. Progression is gated physically: unlocking doors, clearing
  fallen timber and restoring rooms opens new wings and triggers new
  arrivals.

- **Environmental clue-finding.** Before a confrontation, the player
  reads the environment: muddy footprints, broken furniture, wolf
  tracks, pried locks, hidden food caches.

- **Intervention and discipline scenes.** Discovering an infraction
  triggers a framed dialogue scene where the protagonist enforces
  boundaries, teaches a practical skill or administers firm discipline.
  Dialogue choices shape each heroine's growth and change later ambient
  interactions.

- **On-rails and cutscene journeys.** Key transitions play as guided,
  cinematic walks with continuous dialogue: escorting someone through
  the woods at twilight, touring hazardous ruins, facing an external
  threat at the village border.

- **Evolving manor state.** The Hall changes from dusty and boarded up
  to warm and lived in, with characters filling specific rooms and
  forming their own dynamics.

## The protagonist: Silas Fenwick

**Archetype.** The practical woodsman and reluctant lord, in his 30s to
40s. He is now Silas Fenwick, Squire of Fenwick Hall.

**Background.** A self-reliant man who works with his hands, reads
tracks, mends timber and lives simply. He never expected land, title or
dependents. He inherits as a distant, last-resort heir: third or fourth
in line, from a great-uncle who outlived two closer heirs, let the staff
go rather than replace them, and spent his last years shut in the Hall.
Silas arrives to arrears, half-let farms and a house that has forgotten
attention. His earlier life managing other men's land taught him estate
work, but never gave him the final say.

**Title and claim.** Squire, not lord of the manor. His authority is
informal but locally absolute, by custom and tenancy rather than
peerage. Every household in Redmere holds from him as tenant, and any
new building or clearing inside the estate's borders needs his sanction.
That gives him a reason to visit the wood's edge and a moral authority
beyond the written terms.

**Temperament.**

- Exacting and economical with words. Warmth shows in what he does,
  which reads as steadiness to those who need it and withholding to
  those who want more.

- Immovable once decided, because he checks his reasoning thoroughly
  first.

- Dry humour, rare enough to feel earned.

- Takes authority seriously because it is new to him.

- Unimpressed by vanity or excuses, and deeply protective once someone
  is in his care.

**Method.** He walks his grounds regularly to take stock, and answers
moral questions at the estate's edges by presence rather than decree. He
hears an account before he judges, and he will not judge where he holds
no claim.

**Flaws the game can use.** Rigidity once committed, reading silence as
agreement, and mistaking competence in a girl for ease. Each chapter can
make him wrong in a different way while he stays sympathetic.

**Open non-exclusivity.** Silas is openly non-exclusive and says so
plainly and early to each woman. In play, each relationship runs on its
own track, and nothing the player does in one thread lowers another.

## Setting and lore: Redmere, the Hall and the Fenwood

Three names share a root: Fenwick, Fenwick Hall and the Fenwood. The
village deliberately does not, so it reads as older than the family
holding it.

| Place                       | What it is                                                                                                           | Used for                                                                    |
|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Redmere                     | The village, named for the mere beside it. Bakery, the green, Goldie's family home, a vicar, an alewife and a reeve  | Public life: gossip, market, parents                                        |
| Fenwick Hall                | The squire's house. Neglected, in arrears, with a household that forgot what attention felt like                     | Private life: the servants' wing, the study, the West Wing, the upper rooms |
| The Fenwood                 | The wood at the village's edge and beyond, held by the Fenwicks. Granny's cottage, the timberline camp and the creek | Boundary life: tracks, wolves, the forest path                              |
| Walled garden and gatehouse | An overgrown garden, and an isolated tower on the eastern perimeter                                                  | Rapunzel's chapter                                                          |

**Tenancy.** Every household in Redmere, including the bakery, holds
from the Hall. This is why the village registers a young squire's
arrival: his visits, his rulings and the women he takes into his
household are all public business.

**Kinship rumour (open).** The Fenwicks are said to keep an old,
half-serious kinship with the wood. It can stay as local colour, become
part of why Silas reads the wood so well, or be dropped.

**Period.** Pastoral and feudal, with no named century and no fixed
season.

## Cast of characters: the seven heroines

The seven heroines and their roles are unchanged. All are 18 to 21 and
unmarried. Goldilocks, Red and Belle also exist in the Redmere series,
so their histories and temperaments are added below. The other four keep
their original descriptions, because the series has nothing on them.

| \#  | Heroine                | Chapter                 | Role                                                | Core flaw                                                                       |
|-----|------------------------|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1   | Goldilocks             | The Dusty Hall          | Live-in housemaid                                   | Chronic carelessness, daydreaming, treating others' property without boundaries |
| 2   | Little Red Riding Hood | The Forest Path         | Village ward, woods companion and romantic interest | Naivety, distraction, ignoring warnings about forest hazards                    |
| 3   | Belle                  | The Ledger and the Wing | Estate bookkeeper, ward and romantic foil           | Fierce obstinacy, intellectual pride, testing forbidden boundaries              |
| 4   | Cinderella             | The Sooty Kitchens      | Scullery and laundry maid                           | Extreme self-neglect, passive submission to exploitation, sneaking out at night |
| 5   | Gretel                 | The Root Cellar         | Kitchen hand and larder keeper                      | Scarcity-driven distrust, instinctive lying, hoarding food                      |
| 6   | Rapunzel               | The Perimeter Folly     | Ward and walled-garden tender                       | Total worldly ignorance, social naivety, wandering into hazards from wonder     |
| 7   | Snow White             | The Deep Timberline     | Protected manor ward                                | Fatal gullibility, inability to turn away strangers                             |

### 1. Goldilocks

**Name.** Given name Marigold. At her own request Silas calls her
Goldie, and he does. The village and the rest of the household often say
Goldilocks, for the hair.

**Discovery.** Found fast asleep in an upstairs guest bed days after
Silas moves in. She wandered onto his land and found his house instead
of the Bears', leaving half-eaten rations, spilled ink and a broken
antique chair.

**Arc and dynamic.** After disciplining her for the trespass and damage,
Silas learns she has no real prospects and offers her a position as maid
to work off her debt. She takes the offer to her parents and returns
only if she is willing. Her employment is public, and she visits her
parents on her days off. She is his first dependent, and she needs firm
structure to turn scattered daydreaming into mindfulness.

**History (age 18).** Her father keeps a working trade in Redmere, and
her mother runs a household in which her younger siblings fell into step
early. Goldie never did. She has not found a reason a chore mattered
more than whatever lay past the garden wall. Her parents have moved from
scolding to embarrassment to tired resignation, and the village has
watched it happen.

**Temperament.** Curious, quick and unbothered by disapproval in the
moment; it lands later, privately, in ways she would never admit. She
does not defer to authority because it is authority. She needs a reason
and tests whether one exists. Honest to a fault: she does not hide what
she has done, and she argues her case instead of taking a consequence
quietly.

**Resistance.** The most overt of the seven. She defies in the open,
debates, and tries every boundary to see which hold. What holds her is
not firmness alone but the first structure that meets her curiosity
instead of suppressing it.

**Motif.** Just right: a chair, a place at the table, a room that fits
her.

### 2. Little Red Riding Hood

**Discovery.** Encountered off the main forest trail while Silas patrols
the boundary woods, cornered by a lone wolf after straying from the
path.

**Arc and dynamic.** He drives off the wolf and escorts her back to the
village in an on-rails twilight walk, promising her mother he will keep
an eye on her. Over repeated encounters he teaches her woodcraft,
vigilance and safety, with firm correction when she slips, as a
slow-burning romantic bond develops.

**History (age 19).** Her father died young of illness, leaving her
mother to carry the village bakery alone. Red works the counter and the
oven, and carries sweets and supplies to her grandmother's cottage in
the Fenwood, on Silas's land. Girls take no formal apprenticeships, so
her future is uncertain. The village calls her Red for the cloak her
mother dyed for her as a girl, which she still wears past the point of
practicality. Her given name is Rose.

**Temperament.** Warm, generous with her attention and well liked across
the village. Restless under repetition and drawn off the path by
whatever catches her eye. A low, constant unease about her future sits
under her cheerfulness, and she rarely names it.

**Resistance.** Repetition, not rebellion. Her lapses come from
inattention and impatience, not defiance: dawdling on errands, straying,
arriving late. Discipline is about follow-through and presence of mind.
Her own flaws drive what happens, so she reads as a flawed woman in need
of structure and not a victim.

### 3. Belle

**Discovery.** While auditing the previous owner's neglected ledgers,
Silas summons a local merchant to answer for substantial unpaid debts.
The merchant arrives with his adult daughter, Belle, who steps forward
to offer her skills as bookkeeper to clear the debt.

**Arc and dynamic.** Refusing to treat her as a servant, Silas takes her
on as his ward and potential prospect, housing her in fine upper rooms
and dining with her as an equal, on the strict condition that she stays
out of the dangerous West Wing. Her curiosity keeps drawing her to the
locked wing, forcing him to match her wits and assert authority to keep
her safe.

**History (age 20).** She is a merchant's daughter, and only child. Her
mother died when Belle was young enough that she took over the household
accounts out of necessity, and the competence hardened into character.
Her father's luck turned through bad timing, not recklessness, which
makes his debt harder to resent. As Silas's ward she relieves that debt
and reduces her father's costs by keeping her.

**Temperament.** Precise, well read, guarded and deeply proud of being
capable. She handles feeling privately. Being handed over to settle a
debt wounds her pride before it is anything romantic.

**Resistance.** Quiet. She complies correctly and without complaint
while withholding everything she is not asked for. There is little
outright misbehaviour to discipline; the work is getting past her
management, so she feels something in front of him instead of handling
it alone. She may understand the Hall's finances better than Silas does.

### 4. Cinderella

**Name.** Given name Eleanor. Silas uses it. Others in the village and
the household call her Cinders, for the hearth ash she is always found
in, and she answers to both.

**Discovery.** Hired from the village to scrub the soot-choked kitchens
and laundry courtyard, she is found asleep beside a cold hearth,
bruised, exhausted and in rags despite the wages paid to her household.

**Arc and dynamic.** The direct foil to careless Goldilocks. After Silas
finds her stepmother pocketing her wages, he moves her into the Hall
permanently and breaks her habit of working herself to collapse,
enforcing rest, curfews and self-respect.

**History *(age 20)*.** Her father died and left her to a stepmother and
two stepsisters who took her wages and her room, and she kept the
household running in return for a corner by the hearth. Her household
draws the wages Silas pays for her work, and none of it reaches her.
Work became the one place she was safe from criticism, so she does more
of it, and the village has watched her fade without anyone stepping in.

**Temperament.** Patient, dutiful and quiet, with a stubborn kindness
she hides behind work. She does not ask for anything, so people stop
offering. Beneath the exhaustion sits a wish for the village gatherings
she keeps slipping out to, the only place she is a girl her own age and
not a pair of hands.

**Resistance.** Passive. She says yes to everything and complies to the
point of collapse, hiding how tired she is. Her lapses are night outings
and overwork, not defiance. Discipline for her is about rest, curfews
and self-respect, and she guards what she needs far more than she shows.

### 5. Gretel

**Discovery.** Caught raiding the smokehouse and root cellar, and
setting illegal snares in the brush, to feed herself and her sick,
injured brother Hansel after being abandoned in the woods.

**Arc and dynamic.** Respecting her survival instincts, Silas disarms
her snares, gives Hansel light stable work once he recovers, and places
Gretel in the kitchens under strict supervision, teaching her she no
longer needs to steal or lie to be secure.

**History *(age 19)*.** Abandoned in the woods with her brother Hansel
when the household could no longer feed them. She kept them both alive
through winter by trapping, stealing and telling whatever story kept a
door open. By the time Silas finds her, scarcity is how she thinks.

**Temperament.** Sharp, practical and watchful, with a survival instinct
she is proud of. She trusts nothing she has not tested, and she lies by
reflex because honesty has cost her before. Fiercely loyal to her
brother, and quietly funny once she is fed.

**Resistance.** Concealment. She lies, hoards food under floorboards and
sets snares in the brush, and she shows little outright defiance because
she has learned that open defiance gets people hurt. Discipline for her
is about security, so she can stop stealing and lying once she is sure
there will be a next meal.

### 6. Rapunzel

**Discovery.** While clearing the briar-choked eastern perimeter, Silas
inspects an isolated gatehouse tower rented to an eccentric, domineering
older tenant, Gothel, and finds a grown young woman kept confined for
years.

**Arc and dynamic.** After evicting Gothel for unlawful confinement, he
brings Rapunzel into the main grounds. Even more sheltered than Red, she
needs patient, constant guidance in common sense, household rules and
safety, and finds peace tending the walled medicinal garden.

**History *(age 20)*.** Gothel took her to the gatehouse tower as a
child and kept her there for years, the only tenant on the eastern
perimeter. She has read the books Gothel allowed and watched the wood
from a high window, but she has never walked a path or met a stranger on
her own terms.

**Temperament.** Wonderstruck, trusting of the first kindness she meets,
and without any sense of what is dangerous. Gentle and curious, she asks
questions nobody has answered for her. She may start over-compliant and
turn wild once she tastes freedom, and she reads any enclosure as
another tower.

**Resistance.** Low on both counts. She wanders because she is dazzled,
not because she is defying anyone, and she apologises sincerely each
time. Discipline for her is about teaching what freedom is for: common
sense, household rules and safety, with patience rather than severity.

### 7. Snow White

**Discovery.** Deep in the timberlands, charcoal burners and woodcutters
working on his land hand over a young woman fleeing a murderous
stepmother in a neighbouring parish. Only the lord of the manor has the
walls and authority to protect her.

**Arc and dynamic.** Housed in the upper Hall alongside Belle, she tests
his patience whenever her warm heart leads her to unlatch gates or
accept gifts from unvetted visitors, requiring strict security drills as
her stepmother's agents close in.

**History *(age 18)*.** Raised in a great house in the neighbouring
parish, where her stepmother's jealousy of her turned lethal. She fled
with nothing, and the woodcutters who find her treat her as someone to
protect, not someone to question.

**Temperament.** Warm, trusting and quick to love, with a small vanity
about being looked at and liked. She cannot turn away a stranger, a
peddler or a sob story, and she believes the best of people for longer
than is safe. She wants the household to like her, and she gets
competitive with the other women when it does not.

**Resistance.** Low on both counts. She apologises sincerely and repeats
the lapse, because the warmth that causes it is also the best thing
about her. Discipline for her is about security drills and learning to
say no, and it has to leave her kindness intact.

### Village and household cast (series)

Red's mother, the baker, hopeful and practical about her daughter's
future and relieved by any protector. Red's grandmother in the Fenwood.
Goldie's father, mother and younger siblings, unseen but reported on by
Goldie. Belle's merchant father, whose visits are awkward and
transactional at first.

### Ambient vignettes between chapters

- **Goldilocks and Cinderella.** Roommates in the servants' quarters.
  Cinderella covers for Goldilocks's messes until Silas holds Goldilocks
  accountable.

- **Belle and Snow White.** Companions in the upper rooms.
  Sharp-tongued, sceptical Belle grounds gentle, overly trusting Snow
  White.

- **Red Riding Hood and Rapunzel.** Outdoor companions. Red helps teach
  Rapunzel to navigate the village and paths safely.

- **Gretel and Goldilocks.** Scrappy, practical Gretel keeps daydreaming
  Goldilocks honest in the kitchens and pantry.

### Lore notes for the chapters

- **Ch. 1.** Goldie's wandering is why she finds the Hall. Her parents
  and the village watch her change in instalments, and her trade-holding
  family lives in Redmere.

- **Ch. 2.** Red's mother is relieved when Silas brings her home and
  promises to keep an eye on her, and the village starts to read him as
  a protector.

- **Ch. 3.** Belle's father is the debt made flesh. The West Wing
  carries the great-uncle's last years in the Hall.

## Climax, win state and narrative ending

### The trial: the convergence at the West Wing

In the final chapter, two outer threats converge on the Hall at once:
Snow White's vindictive stepmother, backed by local bailiffs challenging
the estate's title, and a pack of starving winter wolves. Silas
coordinates the household, relying on the skills each woman has
developed:

- **Red Riding Hood** tracks and signals the wolf pack's movements
  across the perimeter.

- **Gretel and Cinderella** fortify and provision the ground floor and
  entryways.

- **Goldilocks** spots a structural breach in the servants' stairs
  before it fails.

- **Rapunzel and Snow White** guard the upper safe rooms and the estate
  deeds.

- **Belle** uses the estate ledgers and legal records to outmanoeuvre
  the bailiffs at the front gate.

Entering the long-forbidden West Wing during the crisis reveals the
truth about the previous owner: not a monster, but a lonely, broken man
who failed to anchor his own troubled family and let the house decay in
grief. In the lore he is Silas's great-uncle. Confronting this history
seals Silas's legitimate inheritance, and it answers the title challenge
the bailiffs bring.

### The ending status quo

With the snows clearing, the dilapidated shell is a warm,
self-sustaining community. Fires burn in every hearth. Silas has grown
from a solitary woodsman into a grounded, respected squire, with an
equal partnership and resolved romance with Belle, a close patrol and
romantic bond with Red Riding Hood, and a loyal household of tenants and
wards who respect the boundaries he keeps.

The village's view of him has changed by the end: fair employer, steward
who settles debts, protector, and a landlord who keeps his promises.
Redmere still disapproves of his open non-exclusivity, and the vicar
still says so, but the village now says it across a table he has helped
fill.

## Post-game: The Steward's Year

After the main narrative the game becomes an open-ended free-roam across
all unlocked zones and recurring seasons. Discipline stays a core
pillar, shifting from survival-critical safety corrections to
interpersonal boundaries, habit maintenance and romantic and domestic
push-and-pull.

### Ongoing friction

| Heroine         | Post-game lapse                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Goldilocks      | Old restlessness: sneaking into the West Wing attics, rearranging parlour furniture at midnight, taking unauthorised horse rides deep into the woods. Needs firm reminders of household order and teasing fireside lectures                           |
| Red Riding Hood | Overconfidence: tracking wolves alone or ignoring curfew in the deep timber. Discipline is stern, protective and charged with romantic tension                                                                                                        |
| Belle           | Tests his authority and patience as co-manager and partner: covertly altering ledger rules to prove a point, reading forbidden West Wing texts until dawn, debating him in front of the staff. Needs wit matched and boundaries reasserted in private |
| Cinderella      | Occasionally relapses into overworking herself during harvests                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Gretel          | Still hoards treats or sneaks pantry food out to stray animals and village children                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Rapunzel        | Still wanders off when distracted by rare plants                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Snow White      | Her empathy occasionally leads her to admit unvetted travellers through the front gates                                                                                                                                                               |

### Post-game mechanics

- **The habit drift system.** Each resident has a hidden Compliance and
  Mindfulness value that drifts slowly over in-game days if unchecked,
  bringing back minor clues such as unlatched doors, misplaced ledgers
  and late-night candle glow.

- **Late-night interventions.** Catching a tenant, ward or partner
  breaking curfew or pushing boundaries triggers intimate, repeatable
  vignettes that blend authority, dry humour and affection, depending on
  the player's chosen tone.

### Lore additions to the loop

- **Village days.** Silas's week has a rhythm: village days, Hall days
  and boundary walks. Each place serves different heroines, so the
  pattern spreads his attention across them.

- **Gossip.** Market days, the green and the bakery counter carry news
  of each heroine's arrangement. Goldie's and Red's families react to
  visible change, and Belle's father calls at the Hall.

- **Non-exclusivity.** Each relationship runs on its own track. Nothing
  the player does in one thread lowers another.
